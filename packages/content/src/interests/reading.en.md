I read to understand two things I will never fully understand: ourselves and the universe.

On one shelf sit the Stoics, Seneca, Epictetus and Marcus Aurelius, with Kant, Dostoevsky, Foucault and Borges: how to live, how to think, and what power and society do to us. On the other, Einstein's general relativity, Stephen Hawking and Kip Thorne: black holes, spacetime, and the strange idea that the universe can be understood at all.

The two meet more often than it seems. Both start from a question that sounds simple and turns out not to be, and both reward patience more than cleverness. Some of what they taught me ended up in the essays below.
